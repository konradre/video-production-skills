# The global spec of an ad campaign — decided once, before the first shot

## Delivery and framing

- **Aspect** 9:16, 1080×1920 delivered; generated at the venue's native resolution (480p on the
  established chain) and reconstructed up (`video-finish-qc` § tier).
- **Safe zones**: gags, faces and text clear of the top 250 px and the bottom 350 px (platform UI);
  critical for every text gag. The figures are one platform overlay's at the time they were read
  (`video-finish-qc` PIPELINE.md carries a paid-social spec's) — read the target platform's current overlay first.
- **Lens**: eye-level, mild wide (~28 mm) for scene work; product shots 50 mm, shallow, locked off.
- **The true-size rule**: the product's size is anchored to a body part per framing; a close-up is
  close enough that the actual shape and the actual size of the product's pieces read.
- **Per-clip model cap**: a scene is one continuous action within the venue's clip ceiling (a beat
  longer than the ceiling is two clips cut on a still moment; venues also carry a minimum — 4 s on the
  established chain).
- **Captions** burned in, every spot (sound-off is the default viewing state); the narrator only.

## The two-state grade and the music

When the campaign's joke is a before/after, the grade flips with it: a clean, desaturated *before*
(and every product/display shot) against a saturated, warm *after* — the grade flip is half the joke.
The music follows the same arc: the *before* cue, a hard cut to the *after* cue ON the wipe, the closing
bed under the display and the card. (`look-library`: the ads pair; `spot-audio-assembly` § music.)

## The standing rules (each one closes a filter, a fidelity or a continuity problem at once)

1. **The product and its pieces are DESIGNED, never generated**: clean plates from the generator, the
   pieces composited from the client's own stills (the exact silhouette IS the product — a blob is a
   defect); packaging and the display are designed elements (`designed-elements`). Solves the content
   filter, cross-shot continuity, product fidelity and re-timing in one move.
2. **Text is never generated**: banners, garments, name dings, the card — composited.
3. **The explicit frame is never framed**: the gag plays in reactions — a filter workaround that is the
   funnier cut; an explicit line rides as post audio, never in a prompt.
4. **The signature wipe is in every spot**: the brand transition is the connective grammar that lets
   the spots compile later; it cannot be added to footage that was not cut for it.
5. **Transformation pairs are generated FROM their before-plate** (B from A's keeper frame 0, the same
   camera) or the room reads as a different house.
6. **The fill is SKU-locked** (the fill = the product's loose pieces, where it has any): the fired
   product dictates the colour; every shot that shows pieces names its `ELEM-FILL-*`; a SKU
   reassignment is a client decision and is audited across every shot.
7. **Build the easiest spot first** (zero dialogue, zero filter exposure) to prove the chain — and know
   it proves the chain, not the hard parts; the discriminating tests are a reference call and a
   lipsync shot.

## The sign-off structure (fixed) and its variables

```
   <variable line, first half>     ← varies per spot, by gag fit
        <audio signature>          ← the brand's sound, cut by the wipe (its character varies — the running joke)
   <variable line, second half>
   <brand line>                    ← LOCKED — the one piece the client keeps consistent
   <CTA>                           ← locked
```

The interruption structure is the reusable asset, not any particular words; the closer rides 0.7 s
into the turntable; the card plays its full length with the audio signature's hit on the card's burst.

## Where a spot's runtime comes from

Setup → surprise → product beat → sign-off. The gaps a standalone usually has (no pack shot, no CTA, no
captions) are ~2–3 s of post, not script. Every spot = the gag quip + the decided outro.
