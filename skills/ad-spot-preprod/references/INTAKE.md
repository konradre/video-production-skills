# Intake — from the client's brief to the decisions that shape the build

## The format decision comes first

"One long clip or individual clips?" is a **sequencing** decision mistaken for a **production**
decision. Cut every scene as a standalone vertical spot; every compile comes free out of the same
footage afterwards, and the reverse is not true (a compile's slices carry no setup and no payoff).
Paid vertical is decided in the first 1–2 s: seven spots put seven hooks in market, seven test cells,
and a platform rejection costs one asset. An intro scene written as the frame of a long film is NOT
its own spot until it is re-cut to stand alone.

**Three tiers from one build:** the standalone paid spots (13–28 s) · the capper montage over the
delivered spots (retargeting) · the hero cut of the client's whole film (owned channels). Do not
stretch them — you will be trimming. The "we need more" impulse goes to **alternate hooks** (2–3 opens
of the same spot), not to runtime. "UGC" in a brief usually means short-form vertical ads — confirm
the wording, not the build.

## The client's text is the SSOT

- Read it END TO END before the first prompt; save it verbatim beside the beats file
  (`<SPOT>-SCRIPT-client.txt`); a pasted block in a message is the SSOT.
- The derivation (the shot list) may add framing, coverage and timing — never a character, a beat or a
  line. Additions are proposed as numbered cost lines and the default answer is no (client-literal is
  the DEFAULT, never re-asked). A reaction needs its CAUSE on the track; the gag's logic decides who is
  on screen at the end.
- The client may switch SSOT mid-build to their original script: save it, diff the accepted assets,
  rebuild the beats. A client's full alternate spec ("same bones, different structure") is a NEW spot
  with its own beats; a one-line note on an existing spot is a version bump.
- "What does the script call for in this shot?" is the first question on any doubtful take — the shot
  list quotes the script line per shot.

## The brand kit to collect

| item | ask | example |
|---|---|---|
| the audio signature | the sound at the end of every spot | a signature sound interrupted by the brand's transition, escalating from spot to spot |
| the fixed line | the words the client is attached to | the brand line — locked |
| the variable line | a pile of punchlines to cherry-pick per spot | the line whose halves bracket the audio signature |
| the CTA | where to buy | the marketplace or the site |
| the triad | what it is · what it does · where I get it | the sign-off structure |

The variable line is assigned per spot by gag fit and never reused across spots; the outro is decided
ONCE for the campaign.

## Assets to ask for

- Packaging photos (every SKU, every side); the product itself at true size beside a known object.
- **FLAT artwork** for every SKU and the brand — packaging photos are wrapped and rounded; the card,
  the turntable texture and every designed element need the dielines (raster is fine; ask for the
  full-resolution originals of any listing shot).
- The product's pieces photographed on white — the fill colour per SKU is read off them; each SKU
  fires one fill only; every shot that shows pieces names its element id.
- Any real-person likeness the client wants — and a check that no generated cast member resembles a
  public figure.

## Rulings to get from the client, once

- **Language and platform risk**: flag the risky lines ONCE with the fallback register; the client
  rules. Operator policy: **risqué first**, the fallbacks built and parked, deployed only on an actual
  platform rejection; a rejection deploys the fallback for THAT asset and changes no policy.
- **Rating** (R vs clean) is the operator's call when the client leaves it open.
- **Wardrobe and set** where the script is silent are the operator's.
- An open script question goes to the client as ONE concise ask.

## The decision log

Every decision is logged with WHO made it, the reasoning the production doc omits, and **what would
reopen it** (a channel-mix change, a SKU change, a test result) — so a later pass neither relitigates
settled ground nor misses a real trigger. Costs quoted in a decision are dated; a stale cost is
re-costed before it is cited again. A handoff to another agent or session carries everything gleaned AND the
authorization.
