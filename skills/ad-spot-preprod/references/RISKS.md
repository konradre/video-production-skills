# The risk register — written before the first generation, with each mitigation already in the shot list

| risk | where it bites | the mitigation that is already in the plan |
|---|---|---|
| **content filters** (the big one) | the explicit frame, the product's pieces, the premise once a rewriter restates it | the pieces are a post layer; the explicit frame is never framed (reactions); the explicit line is post audio; a refused scene is re-staged as a silhouette or a tighter reaction crop — never a rewrite of the joke; a refused spoken line is dubbed (`spot-audio-assembly`) |
| **moderation on a word** | a line the venue refuses in-gen | the take says a similar-mouth word; the client's line is dubbed in the character's clone |
| **lipsync** | every on-camera line | decide per line before generating: reaction-led framing (hold on the listener), a wide/over-shoulder where the mouth is not the focus, or the venue that renders dialogue verbatim; the line-deliverer faces the camera |
| **transformation geometry** | every before/after pair | the B plate generated FROM the A keeper's frame 0; the start image = the previous shot's last state |
| **text in frame** | banners, garments, name dings, the card | always composited (`designed-elements`) |
| **packaging fidelity** | any hero shot of the pack | designed turntable/display; or the shot held near-static with the pack composited |
| **likeness** | a generated cast member who resembles a public figure | re-roll the cast ref; a real person's voice as a clone is stated once |
| **platform copy risk** | the vulgar lines | flagged ONCE with the fallback register; the client rules; risqué first, fallbacks parked; a rejection deploys the fallback for that asset only |
| **fill continuity** | every shot with pieces | one particle source per SKU; every shot names its `ELEM-FILL-*`; audited on any SKU move |
| **venue cost at the campaign's shot count** | the whole build | the cost per keeper (not per second) with realistic attempt counts; the Phase-0 test before any scale |
| **the easiest spot proving the chain** | the first spot | know what it proves; pair it with a reference test and a lipsync test |

## The fallback register (built with the originals, parked, never pre-emptively deployed)

| ships as | parked fallback | cost of the fallback |
|---|---|---|
| the risqué line | the toned-down line | an audio swap (trivial; can wait for a rejection) |
| the text gag on a garment | the innocent variant on the same garment | a second generated/designed card — build it in the SAME batch as the original |
| the product-vocabulary lines | none needed | naming the product is unavoidable; it is what the product is |

## UGC compliance — two paths, chosen at intake, re-checked on every version

A creator-style spot carries a disclosure question a product spot does not: whose voice is this, were they paid, and was
any of it made or altered by AI. The path is decided at intake from the persona axis (UGC-GRAMMAR.md § Where lo-fi wins)
and written into the script's disclosure column. The primaries below were read 2026-09-16; a rule that is not in them is
a trade claim, and the last block lists the ones that were circulating.

| path | what applies (primaries) | the mitigation already in the plan |
|---|---|---|
| **A — a real creator, phone-shot, compensated in any way** (payment, a free or discounted product, a commission, early access) | **FTC 16 CFR 255.5**: a material connection the audience would not expect is disclosed clearly and conspicuously; **255.0(f)**: "clear and conspicuous" = difficult to miss, in the VISUAL portion at least, and in BOTH the visual and the audible portions when the endorsement is made both ways; **255.2(c)**: an ad presenting "actual consumers" uses actual consumers in audio and video, or says the persons are not; **255.2(a)–(b)**: the advertiser holds the substantiation for every claim an endorser makes and discloses the generally expected result when the one shown is not typical. FTC Disclosures 101: in the video, not only in the description; hard to miss; simple words ("ad", "sponsored"); the platform's tool "in addition to, not instead of" your own. **Meta** Branded Content Policies: branded content is posted with the branded content tool and the business partner is TAGGED with their permission (the "Paid partnership" label); a partnership ad in Ads Manager runs on the creator's boost permission; an affiliate-link post carries the label without a tag; all other legal disclosures remain the advertiser's. **TikTok** Commercial Content Disclosure: on for any post promoting a brand, product or service; undisclosed commercial content becomes ineligible for the For You feed, the in-app notice must be answered within 24 h, and only the original poster can appeal. | the disclosure lives in the CREATIVE — a spoken line and an overlay in the open, never only the description — and the platform setting is added on top; the creator agreement names payment, usage, whitelisting / partnership-ad permission and an AI-modification clause (a tool that regenerates a delivered face or voice moves the asset to path B); no synthetic label on real footage |
| **B — a generated or significantly AI-modified presenter, voice or scene** | **TikTok Advertising Policies** (Misleading and false content, April 2026): significantly edited media and AIGC are allowed only with the AIGC label OR a clear disclaimer, caption, watermark or sticker of your own; "significantly modified" = beyond minor tweaks — lighting, colour, background removal and denoise are minor; a fully generated image, video or audio, the subject doing or saying what they did not, a cloned voice are not; undisclosed AI → the ad is rejected or restricted. In Ads Manager the toggle "This ad contains AI-generated content" applies to non-Spark ads; a Spark ad follows the Community Guidelines' organic AIGC label; the toggle cannot be unselected after submission without changing the asset, and ANY new campaign — a duplicate included — resets it. C2PA Content Credentials auto-label on upload and cannot be removed; a re-encode commonly strips them, so auto-detection is a backstop, never the plan. **Meta**: ads created or significantly edited with Meta's generative tools or third-party AI carry "AI info" (in About this ad, sometimes beside Sponsored); a mandatory advertiser disclosure exists for social-issue, election and political ads (removal on failure); no commercial-ad mandate is published. **Google Ads**: the AI label setting marks assets created or edited with AI for the "How this ad was made" panel on Search, YouTube and Discover; visible on-ad overlays for campaigns targeting the EU, India and New York; Google-made assets carry SynthID + C2PA; labels cannot be overwritten. **EU AI Act Art. 50** (in force 2 Aug 2026): a DEPLOYER of a system that generates or manipulates image, audio or video constituting a deep fake — content resembling existing persons, objects, places or events that would falsely appear authentic — discloses that it is artificially generated or manipulated, at the latest at first exposure; providers mark outputs machine-readably (the SynthID / C2PA on every generator take). **FTC 255.2(c)** still applies: a synthetic presenter is never presented as an actual consumer. | the label toggle is a line in the finish checklist per platform, re-set on every campaign duplicate; a synthetic persona presents a demo, never a testimonial (UGC-GRAMMAR.md); the disclosure column names the label AND the on-creative disclosure; an AI likeness of a real person needs their written permission (TikTok removes a private figure's likeness and a public figure's false endorsement, labelled or not); the SynthID / C2PA marks are left in place, never stripped |

**The "significantly modified" tripwire.** Running a REAL creator's footage through a tool that regenerates the voice,
the mouth or the face — a lipsync pass, a dub, a face repair — moves a path-A asset into path B. Colour, crop, captions,
denoise and an upscale do not. Ask creators to declare the AI tools they used at delivery.

**What the write-ups claimed and the primaries do not say (read 2026-09-16).** "Meta made Partnership Ads mandatory for all
compensated creator content" and "a non-compliant UGC ad is a Deceptive Practice violation" trace to one undated secondary
source; Meta's published rule is the narrower one above. "TikTok made AI labels mandatory on 21 July 2026" — TikTok's own
2026 ad-policy change log lists no AIGC change; the ad rule dates from the April 2026 refresh. "A verbal disclosure in the
first 3 s of a Reel" is a trade write-up; the FTC's standard is difficult to miss, in the video, visual and audible where
the endorsement is both. IAB's materiality framework (January 2026) is guidance, not a rule. The one date that binds is
2 August 2026 (Art. 50).

**Per-project rulings are named, never inherited.** A project whose operator has ruled on its own disclosure posture (a
faceless affiliate channel, for instance) carries that ruling in its decision log; nothing here changes it.
