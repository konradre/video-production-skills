# The hero shot decides the format; the format decides the pipeline

## The shot the film is built around

Identify it before anything else — for the anagnorisis film it is fifteen seconds, one face, no
dialogue, a slow push-in whose speed is dictated by the performance. **Storyboard it first, generate it
first, build outward from it. If it does not land, the film does not exist, and it is better to know on
day one.** Check the venue's clip ceiling before designing the beat: a 15 s beat past a 10 s ceiling is
two clips cut on a blink or a caught breath, planned as such (the best-engineered hero shots need no
camera move at all — the push is an edit-suite zoom on a locked clip).

## Format

A held close-up on a **photoreal** AI face drifts, morphs and goes dead over fifteen seconds; a
**stylised** face holds because there is no photographic referent to check it against. So the hero
shot decides the format, in this order of weight:

1. the hero shot's survival;
2. the crowded lane (photoreal is the house style of every platform showcase; a painted film is not
   competing with it);
3. likeness risk (a painted face cannot resemble an identifiable person; it also removes lipsync
   exposure — design close-up-weighted, minimal dialogue);
4. a judge's stated taste for illustration over the photoreal.

The honest cost: an animation-literate judge reads mediocre animation faster than mediocre live action.
Not cel, not anime, not the CG house style of the most animation-literate judge — painterly figuration
with photographic camera language.

## 480p → reconstruct is an aesthetic decision, not a cost one

Generate at 480p only and reconstruct up (`video-finish` § tiers): the softness reads as *captured*
on photoreal footage and as *painted* on stylised footage — it strips the clinical digital sharpness
that marks a generated frame, which is the axis judges who reject "render look" actually score. For a
FILM the reconstruction is ONE pass on the locked cut (ads tier per shot; a film upscales the finished
film). Recorded dissent: only viable in close-ups; lipsync artifacts — hence close-up-weighted design.
Inside a contest that bans off-platform generation, the pass runs in-platform, never through an
external upscaler, interpolator or a NLE's neural tools.

## Look

Light the story's own practical source: motivated, **under-lit rather than over-lit, no strong
backlight**, restrained camera, composition over coverage, a face to cut to. Frames dense enough to
reward pausing. Build environments (the subworlds) before shots; continuity between them is the
pipeline's hardest problem (`video-refs-continuity`).

## Sound

All generated where the rules require it (and then in every WIP cut too — no temp track, ever, with a
file → tool → date manifest). **Let the mix crest with the push-in, not before.** No voice-over that
explains the mystery; the revelation is never spoken; two to four lines in the whole film.

## Never

A voice-over that explains · irony, knowingness, mockery-as-cleverness · a sanitised ending when the
brief is a dark fairy tale · editorialising about the medium in either direction · a political or
religious statement (a structure may be mythic; a statement is a disqualifier) · a real person's
likeness or voice, including your own.

## Runtime

Set by the story. A floor is a floor, not a target; every extra minute is more generation, more
continuity risk, and another chance for a weak shot to drag the average down in front of a judge.
