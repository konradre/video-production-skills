# VO — voices, takes, dubs, off-screen lines, word times

## Venue and cost

- **ElevenLabs by raw REST** (`gen_vo.py`): `eleven_multilingual_v2`, `mp3_44100_128`. Billed per
  **character**, per call, with no dry run on the vendor side — `--dry-run` prints the characters each
  job would spend; the plan's `character_count / character_limit` is a free read printed before and
  after so the billed delta is on the record. The starter tier carries a monthly character cap and ten
  voice slots (instant clones yes, professional no). Speech-to-text (Scribe) bills per minute of audio.
- 🔴 **The cost line and the operator's GO come before every TTS, STT, sound-generation or clone call**,
  in the form `video-gen-cost-gate` prescribes, however small the amount.
- An existing output is SKIPPED by name: a take is never regenerated by accident; a new take is a new
  job name (`-b`, `-c`).
- Key: `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` in the environment from the env file that holds it (`KEY=value`, no `export`, no
  quotes — a systemd `EnvironmentFile` rejects `export` and starts a keyless service). Never
  `elevenlabs auth login --with-token` with an `sk_` key (it is filed as OAuth and every call 401s);
  the CLI's 401 text always blames the keyring — probe with `curl …/v1/user -H "xi-api-key: …"` first.

## One voice per role, one model per voice

- The campaign narrator is ONE voice id chosen by the operator, rendered with
  ONE model for the campaign's life: `eleven_v3` renders the same id with a different timbre. Identity
  across regens is audited by ear — same id,
  same model, same settings, and a `seed` when a take must be pinned.
- An **off-screen line is never the narrator**: the client hears the narrator asking a question that
  belongs to the character behind the door. A distinct voice is picked from a reel of shared-library
  voices (added to the account free) against the client's brief (a voice brief in their words), then given the **door treatment** (`--door`: high-pass 110 Hz, low-pass
  1.4–1.8 kHz, a short boxy echo, +1.25 gain).
- A **character's spoken line the venue refused** (moderation on profanity) is
  **dubbed in that character's own cloned voice**: an instant clone from the character's own take lines
  (every sample ≥ 4.6 s), the shot prompted with a similar-mouth word, the real
  line rendered by the clone and placed on the mouth shape (`video-edit-edl`: native muted from the
  onset). The clone id is recorded once and reused. A real
  person's voice as a clone = likeness exposure, stated to the operator ONCE.

## Takes and picks

- Three takes per line (stability / style / seed variants) into a NAMED preview reel with 0.7 s gaps and
  an index (`--preview`); the operator picks **by ordinal from the named file** ("the second one from
  PREVIEW-S03-L2.mp3"). "not bad" is not a pick.
- Steer prosody and language without speaking it: `prev` / `next` = `previous_text` / `next_text`
  (English context — multilingual v2 reads slang as another language without it). Slang is spelled the
  way it must sound (**phonetic prompting**: "lada" / "lotta", never "lot of"), and a spelling spread
  across the three takes finds the reading.
- A quip's variant text changes the captions too; the VO script is a table in the EDL (`text` on every
  line) and is quoted in the review ask.

- **A client line note is a take BATCH, not a fix**: the same words re-voiced (delivery is the note) AND
  the client's alternative wording, context-steered, three seeds per setting, reels to the operator for the
  pick; the picked take's word times spliced in with `--only` so hand patches on the other lines survive;
  the new take placed on the OLD beat (last-word end before the hit; speech start after the cut), and the
  placement verified by envelope NCC (< 15 ms) before the render.

## Where a VO file came from — provenance and the gap floor

- Every placed line records `source`: the TTS job id, the clone id, or `extracted_from: <path> @ <s>`. Lifting a
  line out of a delivered cut is a reasonable act — the client approved that reading — and without the field
  nothing in the record says the file was never separated from the mix it came out of.
- **A VO excerpted from a finished cut is not a stem.** It carries that cut's music bed and the scene's native
  audio inside every line: the new mix shows a second track appearing whenever the voice does, muting the events'
  native audio changes nothing, and every placement instrument validates the file against itself.
- `build_vo_stem.py` reads each line's **gap floor** — the 10th percentile of its 50 ms block levels, L/R
  averaged — before placing it, and refuses a line above `--floor-max`, −40 dBFS by default (measured on one job's
  four lines: −28.9 to −38.0 dBFS lifted from a finished cut, −50.4 to −87.1 for the same lines rendered clean; move
  the threshold only on a new measurement — a recorded voice measured after its repair chain, or another TTS vendor's
  clean files, is that measurement for its project). A line that legitimately carries sound under it — a room
  recording, a treated O.S. line — declares `floor_ok: "<why>"`.

## Word times

- `vo_word_times.py --engine local` — faster-whisper on this machine, free; merges hyphen fragments.
- `--engine scribe` — ElevenLabs Scribe, billed per minute; cost line first.
- **An empty or short timing result stops the run.** A line that comes back with no words, or with fewer words than
  the captions reference, exits non-zero naming the line, and nothing is written — a timing gap is never filled by
  interpolation. Upstream precedent (an explainer kit, 2026-09): a TTS library changed its default boundary type,
  returned no word events, and the captions fell back to character interpolation up to half a second out.
- **`--only L2` when one take is swapped**: a full re-run clobbers hand patches on the other lines
  (a spurious 40 ms "The" came back; three patched splits were undone).
- Whisper mis-times onsets in noisy sections: word times drive the cards; where a line SITS is
  measured by `qc_vo_placement.py` on the delivered file, not by the transcript.

## Recorded voice — the repair order

Generated VO arrives clean; when a RECORDED voice enters a lane (a client's phone take, a room mic), the chain runs in
one order only: surgical repairs first (de-click, de-plosive — an impulse), then the neural denoiser, then loudness.
A network trained on additive noise smears an impulse it is handed instead of removing it, and its output has
arbitrary gain, so loudness measured before it is meaningless (`WolfCut`'s experiments bed, 2026-08, the finding it
kept). Never another order.
